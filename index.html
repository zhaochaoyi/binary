Break-Even Thresholds

Every criterion's break-even focused-asset PoS on one axis. The coloured bands are the dominance regions: below \(p^*_{\mathrm{FSD},L}\) the portfolio first-order dominates, below \(p^*_{\mathrm{SSD},L}\) it second-order dominates, above \(p^*_{\mathrm{SSD},U}\) and \(p^*_{\mathrm{FSD},U}\) the focused asset does, and in between the ranking depends on the criterion. The expected-utility hurdles (log and CRRA) always lie between \(p^*_{\mathrm{SSD},L}\) and \(p^*_{\mathrm{SSD},U}\), with \(\gamma=1\) recovering the log hurdle and larger \(\gamma\) moving the CRRA hurdle up; the loss-probability and target-multiple hurdles lie between \(p^*_{\mathrm{FSD},L}\) and \(p^*_{\mathrm{SSD},U}\). For a homogeneous portfolio with positive expected excess return, \(p^*_{\mathrm{SSD},L}\le p^*_{\mathrm{EIRR}}\le p^*_{\mathrm{SR}}\le p^*_{\mathrm{SSD},U}\), and the two equal-volatility roots bracket these two hurdles. Hover over a dashed line for the definition of its threshold. In the cards below, a value in green means that the portfolio is preferred under that criterion at the current focused-asset \(p^*\), and a value in red means that the focused asset is; the four dominance thresholds are not coloured.

Reading the calculator: a focused asset wins a criterion when its input \(p^*\) exceeds that criterion's break-even \(p^*\) threshold. The thresholds are not meant to agree with each other. For example, expected NPV is a mean-value criterion, loss probability is a downside-tail criterion, Sharpe is a mean-risk criterion, and log and CRRA utility are preference based.
